# Visual Hierarchy
## What it is
Visual hierarchy is the strategic organization of elements to guide the viewer's eye naturally through a path of importance : first, second, third and beyond. It answers the question: _What does the viewer notice, understand, and feel at every single glance?_
which prevents cognitive friction and prevents the eye from wandering aimlessly.

1. First Look (focal point)
The focal point is the hook which commands the highest amount of visual weight, the first thing the viewer sees. 
- Where the eye goes : The region of the highest contrast, largest scale or most vibrant color. Humans look at faces and eyes first in any composition.
- Why : The brain processes faces instinctively. Contrast disrupts the scanning process causing the eye to lock in that point

2. Second Look (secondary details)
After the focal point, the eye gathers context by scanning the secondary elements. Learns the "who, what, where".
- Where the eye goes : Second largest objects, softer contrasts, areas aligned in a natural reading path (Z-pattern or F-pattern)
- Why : The brain craves explanation and narrative and looks for elements that can be related. Viewers follow directional lines, gaze directions of characters, motion trails

3. Third Look (supporting environnement)
After the second look, the micro details appear to the viewer that reward the user for spending time with the piece. 
- Where the eye goes : background elements, texture, shapes, shadows. 
- Why : The eye is naturally led away from the focal point to the edges of the canvas by perspective, lines, boundaries. Provide context and mood.

Tools that artists use to control the viewer's eye : 
- Contrast
- Scale & Proportion
- Leading Lines : environmental cues (roads, branches, light beams, ...) to push the eye to the next area
- Edges & Framing : well-defined edges attract the eye while blurry cause elements to recede
- Negative Space : Surrounding an element with "breathing room" increases the importance
