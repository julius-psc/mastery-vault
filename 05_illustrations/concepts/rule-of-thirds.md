# Rule Of Thirds

## What it is
The rule of thirds is an old guideline that involves dividing a canvas into a 3x3 grid which improves visual hierarchy, and guides the user's gaze naturally accross the interface. 
![[rule-of-thirds.png]]
1. Leverage the intersection "sweet-spots"
Our eyes naturally focus on the intersections of the grid (as shown by the orange above) rather than the dead middle of the viewport. 
- Hero Elements : I should place the main, most important focus of the illustration on one of the four intersections. 
- User Flow : In left-to-right reading, the TOP-LEFT is the most powerful and should be used for the most critical visual hook.

2. Establish a clear focal ratio
Instead of centering a big illustration, I should use 2/3 for the illustration and 1/3 for the content and interactive elements (CTAs, form, etc). 

3. Direct the gaze (visual flow)
Use the natural lines of the illustration to move the user's eye toward the actionable parts of the UI. 
- Gaze direction: if an illustrated character is looking or pointing in a certain direction, I should align that gesture with a horizontal rule-of-third line
- Eye Movement: users typically scan in an "F" pattern so I should try align illustrations along these natural sweeping paths. 

4. Optimize for responsive design
I should avoid hard-coding absolute positioning. Keep everything scalable for different viewports. 

5. Why Rule-Of-Thirds design beats centered
While centering is sometimes necessary, it is predictable design. Applying the rule of thirds feels more intentional in asymmetry, movement and harmony which feels more organic and engaging.
